0 · Establish output truth

A run was observed in which both members' OSD showed near-perfect sync while the actual pictures were about two seconds apart. Until that is explained, every position number this system produces is a measurement of the same possibly-lying quantity.

Blocking — nothing downstream is believed until this passes

The OSD, Player.GetProperties.time, the client's report to the server, the group's drift estimate and the fine-sync residual all agree with each other because they share a source, not because they are right. That is not a detail to fold into a scenario: it is the pass/fail criterion of every other test here.

The reported position is an actuator, not a readout

This matters more than a display bug, because three separate control loops consume that number and act on it:

  • PlaybackController.estimate_position_ms compares the group estimate against player.getTime();
  • PulseScheduler builds its 3 s median residual window from the same number and issues rate pulses to close it;
  • the server's WaitingGroupState computes delayTicks from the position the client reports, and CorrectionPolicy.CannotConverge decides from that whether to keep seeking or hand the member to a rendezvous.
Consequence

A member whose reported position is biased by +2 s is not merely mis-displayed. It will be actively driven two seconds away from the group by the controller that believes it is closing the gap — and the server will watch its corrections "converge" onto the wrong place and stop correcting.

Three candidate mechanisms

All measurable; none decidable from logs, because logs only ever quote the suspect number.

(a) Segment-boundary landing A seek on an HLS transcode lands on a segment boundary, not where it was asked. The engine says so itself in CorrectionPolicy.cs and WaitingGroupState.cs:471. If the client reports the position it requested while playing the segment it got, the OSD is wrong by up to one segment. 3–6 s
brackets the ~2 s seen

(c) Output latency Audio sink buffering, HDMI passthrough to an AVR, and the Bravia's own picture processing all delay what you see and hear relative to what Kodi's clock says. Invisible to every position API. 100–500 ms+

Three channels, in trust order

Every later scenario states pass/fail in channel-1 or channel-2 terms and records the reported-position number alongside, as a second measurement. Where the two disagree, the disagreement is the finding.

Trust 1 — arbiter

Camera, both panels in one frame

Two devices side by side playing the timecode asset; one photo containing both burned-in timecodes. Frame-exact and unfalsifiable — and it is the thing actually complained about. Manual, so it validates the other two rather than doing the work.

Trust 2 — the detector

Screenshot + OCR

OCR the burned-in timecode out of a Kodi screenshot and diff it against Player.GetProperties.time sampled at the same instant on the same device. Needs no camera, no second device, no group — so it runs as preflight everywhere and as a watchdog inside long runs.

Trust 3 — what you hear

Audio cross-correlation

One recorder, both devices audible, cross-correlate the asset's 1 Hz beeps. Sub-millisecond, and it measures sound rather than frames — the only channel that catches sink and passthrough latency.

The detector, precisely

This is the answer to “some way of being able to detect this”:

bias_ms = reported_position_ms − ocr_timecode_ms

That single number is the lie. Measure it per device, per playback path.

Prove the channel before trusting it. On some platforms a Kodi screenshot contains the GUI layer without the video surface, or the reverse. Verify per device that a screenshot of a playing timecode clip actually contains a readable timecode — at Kodi 21 and 22, direct play and transcode, with and without inputstream.tempo in the path. Any device where it does not falls back to channel 1 for that path and is recorded as such. The debug overlay must be off.

Gate 0 exit

  1. Channel 2 proven to capture video on each device/path, or recorded as unavailable there.
  2. bias_ms measured for every device × path cell, with its spread over ≥ 10 samples.
  3. Channels 1 and 2 agree within one frame (~42 ms) on at least one cell per device.
  4. The ~2 s observation reproduced and attributed to (a), (b), (c) or something else. If it will not reproduce, its conditions are recorded and it stays open with the watchdog armed.
  5. A bias watchdog exists — a periodic screenshot+OCR check any run can enable, alarming when bias_ms leaves its calibrated band.

If bias_ms is non-zero and stable, apply it to every reported position before comparing members. If it is non-zero and unstable, fine sync cannot be trusted to close anything on that path — and that is a finding with more weight than any scenario below.

Root cause — hot join doubles the joiner's start position

Reproduced on the reported configuration: Bravia playing, Pixel cold-joining, AV1 content. Root-caused to one line. Detail in docs/hotjoin-position-doubling.md.

Confirmed defect

A member cold-joining a Playing group is told to start at group position + elapsed-since-the-queue-last-changed. Because the queue is normally set at position 0, that is almost exactly double the real position. The joiner then cuts backwards by the difference.

Group had playedJoiner started atError
11.2 s27.0 s~16 s
271.6 s546.0 s~274 s
283.9 s570.0 s~286 s

The 11 s run is the discriminator: the error tracks elapsed-since-queue-change, not the position, so this is not a fixed factor of two. syncwatch caught the long case live as DIVERGE BRV vs PXL: -274423ms.

The two halves disagree about which instant they describe

Server (Group.cs :: GetPlayQueueUpdate) extrapolates StartPositionTicks to now, but stamps the update with PlayQueue.LastChange — when the queue last changed, which on a long-running group is far in the past. This matches stock Jellyfin, so it is not a plugin divergence.

Client (kofin :: _apply_play_queue) pairs the two and extrapolates forward from LastUpdate, adding the whole elapsed playback a second time: 

# Position reference: extrapolate from LastUpdate while playing.
reference_ms = last_update if last_update is not None else self.server_now_ms()
self.playback.set_reference(start_ticks, reference_ms, is_playing)

The fix is the fallback already on that line. The position is extrapolated to send time, so its reference instant is now; LastUpdate belongs to the queue-version dedup done twenty lines earlier, which is untouched by the change. 

reference_ms = self.server_now_ms()

Why it self-heals, and why that is not enough

PositionBeacon (5 s) and every command carry a correctly paired position and When, so the member converges within seconds — measured settled state after the hot join is +36 ms screen-to-screen, with the API agreeing. What the viewer sees in between is the joiner starting far ahead and then cutting back by 15 s, or 274 s, or landing past the end of shorter content.

Which paths reach it

  • Cold hot join — the reported case, joiner not already playing.
  • Rendezvous — routes through BeginHotJoin and the same snapshot path.
  • Any snapshot applied while phase == "loading", where _apply_snapshot returns early and leaves the start position to the queue path.
  • Not a re-join while already playing the same item — that takes the adopt branch. Measured landed 230.3s, wanted 230.4s (-42ms).

Fix 2 — the unpause cut fine sync could have nudged

Reported as "skips smaller in magnitude than the fine sync threshold, particularly when un-pausing". Confirmed, root-caused to an asymmetry inside one file, fixed and measured.

Confirmed defect

The unpause path seeked for anything past UNPAUSE_ALIGN_MS (100 ms) — twice, once at arm time and once at the resume — while _align_after_resume, on the other side of the same resume, handed any residual inside the pulse budget to fine sync instead: "a skip is for gross errors". So a few-hundred-millisecond offset got a visible cut on the way in that would have been smoothed on the way out.

The fix

Apply the rule the file already had to both aligns on the way in, gated on tempo.can_close(). That gate is already correct as it stands: the scheduler drops its tempo file both when an item is not routed through inputstream.tempo and when a pulse is not confirmed, so it answers False exactly when nothing can be nudged — an unrouted item still seeks.

Only in the synced phase. A waiting_ready start — a barrier restart or a hot join — is the one chance to begin exactly on the group position, and its offsets are the large ones (1.0–1.7 s measured); handing those to the scheduler would trade one cut for twenty seconds off-rate.

Measured, four pause/unpause cycles

MemberOffsets at unpauseBeforeAfter
BRV-161, -178, -305, -236 mstwo seeks eachleft to fine sync
PXL-168, -202, -210, -255 mstwo seeks eachleft to fine sync

Every offset was past the 100 ms threshold and well inside the 5 s pulse budget. The pulses closed them accurately — moved -84ms (wanted -78ms), moved -97ms (wanted -85ms) — and the screens sat +85 ms apart afterwards with no cut at all.

No regression on the start path. A cold hot join still seeks: [ syncplay/align ] +1179ms to the start position, both members landing at 104.2 s. Worth noting the offsets are consistently negative (~-200 ms) on both devices — members sit slightly ahead of the scheduled start position at unpause, which is a systematic effect of the pause-time spread rather than drift.

R-G — what a real v1 client found

Run with jellyfin-web (Firefox, 10.11.11) joining a live Bravia + Pixel group. This one group produced three server-side defects that nothing else in the exercise could have surfaced, and all three needed a genuine v1 client rather than a synthetic one.

Three defects, and they compound

Defect 1 sets the flag that lets defect 2 wipe the member's explicit opt-out, and defect 3 means nothing was ever going to pull it back. Observed end state: the group believed all three members were synchronised participants while one sat 217 s away.

1 — rendezvous is not gated on the member's protocol version

PathGateCorrect?
SyncPlayManagerV2.cs:761 (wait timeout)group.IsV2Member(session.Id) && HotJoin != falseyes
WaitingGroupState.cs:483 (correction)context is IGroupStateContextV2no

The second tests whether the group is a v2-capable context — always true with this plugin — not whether the member is v2. It reaches BeginHotJoin → SendWireUpdate(session, "StateSnapshot", …), and SendWireUpdate (Group.cs:556) has no version check, unlike ResyncSession (583) and the beacon (680). So a v1 client is sent a v2-only message, against spec §2 and the coexistence guarantee the shadowed-manager architecture rests on. It also bypasses the HotJoin config gate the other path honours.

Intermittent: CorrectionPolicy always grants the first correction (attempts < 2), so this is reached only when a v1 client needs a second one. Join #1 did; join #2 did not.

2 — an explicit IgnoreWait is silently cleared

if (!isBuffering && value.IgnoredByTimeout)
{
    value.IgnoredByTimeout = false;
    value.IgnoreGroupWait = false;   // clears the member's *explicit* choice too
}

One field carries two meanings — "the group gave up on you" (BeginHotJoin, MarkIgnoredByTimeout, SetMemberDisconnected) and "you asked not to be waited for" (SetIgnoreWait) — so clearing the first clears the second. Watched happen three times; the clearest was a member shown [buffering spectator] at 17:03:12 and [buffering] at 17:03:14, wiped inside two seconds.

3 — a member adrift while the group is Playing is never corrected

Corrections live in WaitingGroupState, so none fire while the group plays. A v2 member self-corrects from the 5 s PositionBeacon; a v1 member has neither, so once adrift it stays adrift. Measured: web client at 155.0 s against both Kodi members at 372.5 s, with zero corrections logged across the gap.

A v1 member's real on-screen offset

A separate finding — not the reported hot-join case

Capturing the jellyfin-web window and OCRing the burned-in timecode — interleaved so the web client's screen position is interpolated to the instant the Bravia's frame was taken — the web client sits 2363 ms behind on screen while the API reports the gap as ~1.1 s. Stable across samples: −2363, −2390, −2272 ms.

Two things compound. jellyfin-web reports position on a ~3 s cadence (421.0, 424.0, 428.0, 430.758967… — repeated values), so any single read is up to a report interval stale and understates the gap. And nothing closes it: corrections live in WaitingGroupState, and a v1 member has no PositionBeacon to self-correct from — so the offset is fixed rather than drifting, which is exactly how it was described from the sofa.

A constant gap that nothing corrects, under-reported by the only number a viewer can see. It shares the ~2 s magnitude with the originally reported case but is not the same thing: that report was Bravia joined by Pixel on AV1 over a hot join, kofin only, and is attributed to the hot-join position doubling fixed in eeac6ee.

And the standing cost of a mixed group: hot join is v2-only, so every v1 join takes the classic barrier. The v2 members paused 3.2 s on one join and 9.7 s on another, bounded by the 10 s GroupWaitTimeout. Not a defect — the price of coexistence, and worth stating plainly.

Gate 0 — results, 26 Aug

Run against minipie 10.11.11 / plugin 10.11.0.4 / kofin 0.21.0, with L22 and TAB on the 10-minute h264 timecode asset. Full detail in docs/shakedown-gate0-results.md.

Verdict — partially passed

The output-truth channel is established and calibrated on both available devices. The ~2 s divergence did not reproduce in any configuration tested — the worst steady-state disagreement measured is 275 ms. But the mechanism that would produce it is confirmed present, and it grows with the transcode path and with a seek.

Screen vs API, measured

ConfigurationOn screenAPI saysDisagreement
both DirectStream + fine sync-195 ms-335 ms+139 ms
L22 DirectStream+tempo, TAB Transcode-75 ms-350 ms+275 ms
same, immediately after a group seek-416 ms-687 ms+271 ms

Per-device bias (reported − screen) is -264 ms on L22 and -384 ms on TAB, so the direct/direct case is predicted at +120 ms and measured at +139 ms. The transcode adds roughly a further 135 ms. The transient at the seek reached -1225 ms before settling — caught live by syncwatch, not found afterwards in a log.

kofin already knows the transcode lands off. The seek made it explicit on the transcoding member: [ syncplay/landed ] 420.3s, wanted 420.0s (+320ms) transcoding then [ syncplay/align ] -317ms carried: transcoding. It measures the landing error and carries it rather than re-seeking — reasonable, since a second seek lands somewhere else again. That carried offset is real displacement the reported position never shows, and it is the confirmed contributor behind the +275 ms.

What the channel actually needed

  • Kodi's own screenshot is unusable on L22 — CCaptureService::Fail ... capture request failed (content 0, 0x0), reproducibly. Capture has to come from outside Kodi: import -window on X11 (~140-210 ms), adb screencap raw on Android (~500 ms; the -p PNG encode more than doubles it).
  • min(R,G,B), not greyscale. The timecode is white over saturated colour bars; greyscale makes the bars light and every threshold blew the crop to white. Min-channel survives only where all three channels are high.
  • tesseract returns nothing on a clean crop that runs to the image edge — it reads perfectly once a white border is added.
  • Two false readings before the estimator was right: +500 ms (OCR inside the timed bracket — exactly half the ladder's runtime) and +985 ms (fetch inside it). Where in the capture call the frame is sampled was then measured, by running one device at two window sizes: the start model agrees to 16 ms, the midpoint model disagrees by 359 ms.

Incidental findings

  • Two sessions per device, one of them inert. kofin authenticates as Client="Kofin". A REST call with Client="Kodi" and the same token creates a second, websocket-less session: the group forms, the server advances Idle→Waiting→Playing, and no client hears a word. Silent from the server side. spgroup members now prints which session groups reach.
  • The wait-timeout path fired for real while that was happening — both inert members went IsBuffering=true, IgnoreGroupWait=true and the group played on without them. D6 behaviour, observed by accident.
  • Real pings are 1-34 ms, not 0, so the Math.Max(delayTicks, DefaultPing) unit bug does not bite on this LAN. Members do start at the Ping = 500 default until time sync runs.
  • TAB's clock offset is -523.9 ms against the server (stable, rtt 3.9 ms); L22's is -3.9 ms. Being absorbed correctly — but TAB's whole group estimate rests on that one measurement.
  • F5 passed incidentally: queue 4.0→1.0 s at join, back to 40 with an empty restore record at leave.
  • adb shell grep 'a\|b' silently matches nothing — cost a wrong conclusion that a single-pattern re-run reversed.

Where the 2 s is most likely to be

  1. The Bravia's 16 s queue. Both tested devices run 4.0 s natively. The queue_secs correction there is four times larger, and OMEGA_QUEUE_SECS = 8.0 is the silent fallback whenever the RPC read fails.
  2. A worse transcode landing error. +320 ms on a LAN with a fast server scales directly with segment length and encode-start time.
  3. A slow link — wanshape is built and verified but has not yet been pointed at a member.

3 · Tooling

3.1 · wanshape — the WAN proxy

A layer-4 TCP proxy. Point one member's serverAddress at it and that member alone becomes remote; the rest of the group keeps its LAN. Built and verified — tools/wanshape.py.

Layer 4 rather than an HTTP proxy because kofin's control channel (the SyncPlay websocket) and its media channel (the range/HLS GETs) are the same host and port, and the websocket stops looking like HTTP after the upgrade. Forwarding bytes shapes both with nothing to parse. Not tc netem, which shapes a whole interface — it cannot make one member remote while its group stays local — and which needs root an unrooted Android TV does not offer. 

tools/wanshape.py --listen 0.0.0.0:8099 --target 192.168.1.167:8096 \
                  --control 127.0.0.1:8098 --rtt 300 --down 3000

tools/wanshape.py --send 'stall 12'       # hold data, sockets stay up
tools/wanshape.py --send 'blackhole 120'  # cut connections, refuse new ones
tools/wanshape.py --send 'down 800'
tools/wanshape.py --send status

Two injectors, because the engine has two paths

blackhole N connections aborted, new ones refused socket dies with no close → SocketLiveness (60 s) → DisconnectedGracePeriod (90 s) → reconnect snapshot

3.2 · syncwatch — watching a run live

Discipline, not tooling

A run is watched, not autopsied. The ~2 s divergence in Gate 0 survived an entire session precisely because everything was read back from logs afterwards — by which time the only surviving evidence was the number that was lying. Every scenario below runs with syncwatch attached and its events in front of you, not started alongside the run and read at the end.

It samples every member over JSON-RPC and the server over /SyncPlay/List, reconciles them, and emits a line the moment anything is worth knowing. Every sample also goes to JSONL for the post-mortem; stdout carries only events. 

tools/syncwatch.py \
    --server http://192.168.1.167:8096 --token-from <settings.xml> \
    --member L22=127.0.0.1:8080 \
    --member TAB=192.168.1.150:8080,adb=192.168.1.150:42753,bias=-180 \
    --tolerance 250 --jsonl run.jsonl --ocr '<ocr command for {member}>'

Three properties make it usable during a run rather than after one:

Property 1

Silence is never success

A heartbeat is emitted on a fixed interval regardless of activity, so a dead poller, a hung Kodi and a healthy quiet group cannot look the same. This is exactly the failure mode of a plain background shell, and the reason one is not good enough here.

Property 2

Every terminal state emits

LOST when a member stops answering, SERVER when the group list errors, STATE on any transition. A watcher that reports only good news is indistinguishable from a watcher that has died.

Property 3

It carries the Gate 0 channel

--ocr reads the burned-in timecode back off a screenshot and reports BIAS whenever a member's reported position drifts from what its own screen shows. Had it been running, the ~2 s would have announced itself instead of being spotted by eye.

Event kinds: START STATE GROUP DIVERGE INBAND BIAS OCR? LOST BACK SERVER HB STOP. A calibrated bias=<ms> per member is subtracted before members are compared; the raw figure stays in the JSONL.

Verified on this rig: members reachable and unreachable, server reachable, heartbeat, JSONL, and the LOST path on a deliberately dead member.

4 · Preflight, per device

  1. inputstream.tempo ≥ x.4.1. Below that the add-on drops the first packets after a seek — the cause of the PXL AV1 wedge — and TempoSession.begin() refuses to arm. Confirm from the log that fine sync armed, rather than silently declining.
  2. videoplayer.usedisplayasclock OFF. The drift gate measured up to +42 763 ppm of rate error with it on (4.3 % on the Bravia — the PAL speed-up), against a few hundred ppm free-running with it off. Fine sync's give-up path exists for exactly this and will fire.
  3. Record videoplayer.queuetimesize before and after — 40 (4.0 s) on desktop and Tab, 160 (16.0 s) on the Bravia. Check syncPlayQueueRestore is empty at start; a non-empty one means a previous session died mid-run. This is hypothesis (b)'s primary evidence.
  4. Debug logging on, on-screen overlay off — the overlay corrupts channel 2.
  5. Screen-off timeouts longer than the run, screensaver None, and on Android svc power stayon true while charging. Record and restore.
  6. Same Jellyfin user on every member for the whole exercise — user-level playback settings change the transcode decision.
  7. Note PXL thermals; an hour of AV1 will throttle it, and that is a real-world condition, not an artefact.
  8. syncwatch running and its events visible before the scenario starts — not started alongside it, and never left to be read back at the end.

On an all-LAN group every member may report ping 0, and WaitingGroupState.cs:540 floors delayTicks against DefaultPing's raw 500 — which is 500 ticks, 0.05 ms, not 500 ms. The clamp only bites when the highest ping is 0, and then the intended 500 ms recovery grace becomes 0.05 ms. Record the reported ping values on a quiet LAN; if they are 0, this is reachable in normal use. (Upstream Jellyfin's line, verbatim, in both 10.11 and v12 — not introduced here.)

6 · Transcoding assessment

Correction to the brief

Fine sync cannot currently be enabled for transcoded playback, and no setting turns it on. plugin.video.kofin/lib/kofin/plugin/play.py:54 reads TEMPO_METHODS = frozenset({"DirectPlay", "DirectStream"}). tempo_route() returns None for anything else, so no inputstream.tempo properties are stamped, the claim carries no Tempo, and PulseScheduler never arms for that item. The fine-sync doc gives the reason: the add-on's HLS path is unqualified.

So a transcoding member gets command-only sync — and that is the floor this exercise must measure rather than assume away.

IDScenarioWhat it measures
T1forced transcode, steady state, no commandshow far a command-only member drifts over 30 min, in channel-1/2 terms
T2group Seek to a transcoding memberreload_current_item — the seek path restarts the stream at the target because an in-stream seek cannot be accurate. Measure the gap it leaves and how it closes.
T3load allowance convergence_load_allowance_ms measured ~9 s for a transcode start, EMA at 0.5, capped at 15 s. Confirm it converges within one item, and that the first item of a session is the bad one.
T4T3 under wanshapea throttled link lengthens the load, so the allowance grows — does it stay inside the cap and still aim correctly?
T5transcode → rendezvousthe motivating case: correction cannot converge, so the member should be rendezvoused rather than seeked repeatedly
T6mixed: one direct member (fine sync armed) + one transcoding member (command-only)the realistic bad case, and the one most likely to reproduce the ~2 s complaint
T7Gate 0 (a), specifically: does a transcoding member report the position it requested while playing the segment it got?highest value the leading hypothesis for the original observation

T7 is the highest-value single test in this document. If it confirms, the fix is on the client — report the position actually playing, not the one requested — and it also explains why the server's corrections appear to converge onto the wrong place.

The open question this exercise should answer, not assume: is extending inputstream.tempo to the HLS path worth doing? That is a prerequisite study on the add-on side, not something this shakedown can switch on. What it can supply is the number that decides it — T1's measured command-only drift. If command-only holds a transcode inside a frame or two, the extension is not worth building; if it does not, T1 is the justification.

7 · Known hazards

  • pkill -f wanshape matches the shell running it and kills your own session. Use the pidfile. (Cost a shell during development.)
  • JSON-RPC setting writes are not saved to disk by Kodi, so a queue shortening can survive a clean exit with no record. Check syncPlayQueueRestore before and after every run.
  • An empty videoscreen.whitelist (Kodi's default) disables mode switching entirely, and on Android the display mode is the platform's decision anyway — so a content/refresh mismatch is not configurable away on this hardware. Keep usedisplayasclock off and the question does not arise.
  • The debug overlay corrupts channel 2. Off before every screenshot.
  • PXL thermal throttling on long AV1 runs — sample dumpsys thermalservice alongside, and do not mistake it for a sync defect.
  • adb shell escaping silently answers zero on some forms; verify each command's output rather than its exit code.
  • A device that blanks mid-run reads as a hung Kodi to any poller.

9 · Exit criteria

  1. Gate 0 passed: output truth established, bias_ms known per device × path, and the ~2 s observation attributed — or explicitly left open with its conditions recorded.
  2. Every R-A / R-B / R-C cell passes in channel-1/2 terms on at least L22 + TAB + WEB.
  3. D6/D7 demonstrate the rendezvous fires at the wait timeout and not before; D8/D9 demonstrate the disconnect grace and rejoin.
  4. E2 demonstrates the negative case — a slow but converging member is not rendezvoused.
  5. G1–G4 pass with a v1 member present throughout.
  6. Transcoding yields a measured command-only drift figure, and a yes/no on T7.
  7. R-I complete for every available device, with the PXL/AV1 cell clean.
  8. H8 soak completes with the bias watchdog quiet — and quiet is only meaningful because syncwatch heartbeats throughout, so a silent watcher cannot be mistaken for a synchronised group.
